Posture and hand shape

Before the first note: how to sit, and how to hold the hand. Get these two right and everything after them gets easier, and kinder to the body.

Set up the stool

Two things decide everything: how high the stool is, and how far it is from the keys. Fix those first, then sit tall, like a puppet on a string.

{{svg:sit-good}}
  • Height: the forearm is level with the keys.
  • Distance: the elbows sit just in front of the body, open a little to the side.
  • Front half of the stool, feet flat on the floor, facing middle C.
  • Feet don't reach? Put a footstool or a sturdy box under them.

Four ways it goes wrong

{{svg:sit-low}}

✗ Stool too low

The forearm slopes up to the keys and the shoulders hunch. Raise the stool.

{{svg:sit-high}}

✗ Stool too high

The forearm slopes down onto the keys. Lower the stool until the forearm is level.

{{svg:sit-close}}

✗ Too close

The elbows are squashed behind the body. Slide the stool back a little.

{{svg:sit-far}}

✗ Too far

The arms reach out ahead of the body. Slide the stool closer.

Let the arm carry the hand

Fingers press the keys, but the arm takes the hand where it needs to go. The hand keeps its shape the whole way. These six warm-ups take a few minutes at the start of practice.

  1. Rag dollLet both arms hang heavy, shake them loose from the shoulders, then float the hands up to the keys. Shoulders stay down.
  2. Knuckle bridgeAll five fingertips rest on C D E F G. Press one key slowly to the bottom and hold for three. The knuckles stay up.
  3. Arm glidePut the hand shape on middle C and carry it up to the next C, and the next. The fingers don't walk: the arm carries them.
  4. Elbow wingsHands still on the keys, let the elbows float out to the side and back. Stop in the middle, about a fist from the body.
  5. Wrist balloonImagine a balloon tied to the wrist. It lifts the wrist while the fingertip stays on the key, then the wrist floats back down to level.
  6. Arm dropLift the whole arm, keep the hand shape, and let it drop into a key on finger 3: firm fingertip, soft wrist, a full round sound.

A round hand

Let the arms hang, then lift the hands as if holding a small ball. That round shape is the one to keep on the keys.

{{svg:hand-0}}
  • Knuckles up, like a bridge. They are the highest point.
  • Wrist level with the forearm, not dropped and not hunched.
  • Play on the fingertip pad, with the fingers curved down.
  • The thumb plays on its side corner, like a boat tipped on its side.

Five faults, and the fix for each

{{svg:hand-1}}

✗ Flat fingers

The key is played with the soft middle of the finger. Fix: hold a small ball, or a mandarin, and put that round shape on the keys.

{{svg:hand-2}}

✗ Knuckles collapsed

The bridge has fallen in, lower than the wrist. Fix: the knuckle bridge warm-up, with the knuckles staying up.

{{svg:hand-3}}

✗ Tip joint caving in

The last joint bends backwards as the finger plays. Fix: play slowly on the tip, press down and hold. The tip stays firm, like a little pillar.

{{svg:hand-4}}

✗ Wrist dropped

The wrist sinks below the keys and the hand has to push up. Fix: the wrist balloon, floating up to level.

{{svg:hand-5}}

✗ Wrist too high

The wrist hunches up and the fingers poke straight down. Fix: let it sink until elbow, wrist and knuckle make a straight line.

Finger numbers

Every finger has a number, 1 to 5, starting from the thumb. It's the same on both hands, so both thumbs are 1 and both little fingers are 5. Teachers say "put finger 2 on D" all the time, so the numbers need to be automatic before reading fingering on the page.

{{svg:numbers-0}}{{svg:numbers-1}}
1 Captain Thumb2 Pointer3 Tall Middle4 Shy Ring5 Baby Pinky

A quick game: hold both hands up, wiggle each finger as you say its number, then call out "Show me left hand, finger 3!" and swap.

A quick posture check

The same check every time, so you can see it improve. Print this page, or just run through it before practice.

Sitting

  • Stool height: forearm level with the keys
  • Distance: elbows just in front of the body
  • Sitting on the front half of the stool
  • Feet flat (or on a footstool)
  • Back tall, leaning slightly forward
  • Shoulders down and loose

Arms

  • Elbows open, a fist's width from the body
  • Forearm level, not sloping up or down
  • Wrist level with the forearm

Hands

  • Knuckles up, like a bridge
  • Fingers curved, holding a ball
  • Playing on the fingertip pads
  • Tip joint firm, not caving in
  • Thumb plays on its side corner

Moving

  • The arm carries the hand along the keys
  • The hand keeps its shape while moving
  • No tension: the arm could wobble freely

Common questions

How high should a piano stool be for a child?

High enough that the forearm is level with the keys. If the feet then don't reach the floor, put a footstool or a sturdy box under them rather than lowering the stool.

Should the wrist be high or low?

Neither: level with the forearm. A dropped wrist makes the hand push up; a high wrist makes the fingers poke straight down.

How far from the piano should you sit?

Close enough that the elbows sit just in front of the body, open a little to the side. Squashed elbows mean too close; arms reaching out mean too far.
