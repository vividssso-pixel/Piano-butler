Chapter 6 of 26 · Reading notes

Ledger lines

Notes don't stop at the edge of the staff. Short extra lines — ledger lines — are the rungs of a ladder that lets them climb out: up into the sky over the treble staff, down into the sea under the bass staff.

Written by Clara Sohyun Park · Updated 11 October 2026

From the teacher

Three things go wrong with ledger lines again and again: counting the rungs wrong, reading a note under the treble staff as if it belonged to the bass staff, and drawing the lines too long or too far apart.

So we do both: first count out from the staff — line, space, rung — and then learn a few ledger notes by heart, five Cs, so that every other ledger note is only a step or a skip away.

A rung for one note

When a note is too high or too low for the five lines, we draw a short line just for that note: a ledger line (also spelled leger). Middle C sits on one — in this book it is Princess Clara's rung. The pattern simply carries on outside the staff: line, space, line, space.

{{svg:ledger-line}}

Drawing one neatly: a ledger line is a little wider than the note, the same distance apart as the staff lines, with no gaps back to the staff — and never one more than the note needs.

Up into the sky

In this book the treble staff is Queensland, the Queen's land. Start on its top line, F. Just above it, in the space, is G. Then rung 1 carries A, the next space B, and rung 2 carries C — Sky C. One letter a step, just like inside the staff, up to rung 3: E.

{{svg:sky}}

Down into the sea

The bass staff is Kingsland, the King's land. Climb down from its bottom line, G. Just under it is F; rung 1 carries E, the next space D, and rung 2 carries C — Sea C. One letter a step, backwards: G F E D C, down to rung 3: A.

{{svg:sea}}

Between the lands: the same key, two ways

The rungs between the two staves are borrowed. The A on rung 2 under the treble staff is the same key as the bass staff's top line, A; the E on rung 2 over the bass staff is the same key as the treble staff's bottom line, E. Middle C sits on one rung, seen from either staff. The same key can be written two ways — so each hand can stay in its own staff.

{{svg:between}}

Five Cs, from the sea to the sky

From low to high there are five Cs, an octave apart: Sea C on two rungs under the bass staff, Kingsland C in the bass staff's second space, Clara's C (middle C) on its rung, Queensland C in the treble staff's third space, and Sky C on two rungs over the treble staff. Every ledger note in this chapter is a step or a skip from Sea C, Clara's C or Sky C.

{{svg:five-cs}}
Teacher's tip

Five minutes at home: play the five Cs from the sea to the sky, naming each one; then the C hop — point to a ledger note, find the nearest C, step or skip, name it, play it. The drill for this chapter does the rest, a little every day.

My five checks

Show each one at the piano.

The rungs

  • I can point to a ledger line and say what it is
  • I can climb out of the treble staff: G, A, B, C, D, E
  • I can climb out of the bass staff: F, E, D, C, B, A

Reading fast

  • I can read the rungs between the staves, both ways
  • I can find all five Cs fast, from the sea to the sky

Common questions

What is a ledger line?

A short line drawn above or below the staff for one note that is too high or too low for the five lines. The line-space pattern carries on through ledger lines exactly as it does inside the staff. "Leger line" is another spelling of the same thing.

How do you read ledger lines quickly?

First count out from the edge of the staff — line, space, ledger line — one letter a step. Then learn a few ledger notes by heart, such as the C two ledger lines above the treble staff and the C two ledger lines below the bass staff, and read the notes near them as a step or a skip away.

How do you draw a ledger line?

Draw it a little wider than the note head, the same distance apart as the staff lines, with no gaps back to the staff. Draw only as many as the note needs: a note just above or below the staff needs none.

Why is the same note written in two places?

Between the treble and bass staves, a note can be written with ledger lines under the treble staff or on the bass staff (or with ledger lines over the bass staff). It is the same key on the piano. Writing it in the hand's own staff keeps each hand in one place on the page.
