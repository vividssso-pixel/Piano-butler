Ledger lines

Notes don't stop at the edge of the staff. Short ledger lines are little ladders out of it: up into the sky over the treble staff, down into the sea under the bass staff.

Written by Clara Sohyun Park · Updated 13 October 2026

From the teacher

Three things go wrong with ledger lines again and again: counting the ledger lines wrong, reading a note under the treble staff as if it belonged to the bass staff, and drawing the lines too long or too far apart.

So we do both. First count out from the staff: line, space, ladder. Then learn a few ledger notes by heart, five Cs, so that every other ledger note is only a step or a skip away.

A little ladder for one note

A note too high or too low for the staff gets a short line of its own: a ledger line. Its other spelling is leger. Middle C sits on one, Clara's ladder in this book. The pattern goes on outside the staff: line, space, line, space.

{{svg:ledger-line}}

Draw a ledger line a little wider than the note, the same distance apart as the staff lines, with no gaps back to the staff. Never one too many: a note in a space gets no lid.

Up into the sky

Start on the treble staff's top line, F. The space above is G. Ladder 1 is A, the next space B, and ladder 2 is C, high C. One letter a step, up to ladder 3: E.

{{svg:sky}}

Down into the sea

Climb down from the bass staff's bottom line, G. The space below is F. Ladder 1 is E, the next space D, and ladder 2 is C, low C. One letter a step, backwards, down to ladder 3: A.

{{svg:sea}}

Between the staves: the same key, two ways

The ladders between the two staves are borrowed. The A on ladder 2 under the treble staff is the same key as the bass staff's top line, A. The E on ladder 2 over the bass staff is the same key as the treble staff's bottom line, E.

Middle C sits on one ladder, seen from either staff. The same key, written two ways, lets each hand stay on its own staff.

{{svg:between}}

Five Cs, from the sea to the sky

Five Cs sit an octave apart: low C two ladders under the bass staff, bass C in its second space, middle C on its ladder, treble C in the treble staff's third space, and high C two ladders over the treble staff.

Fold the grand staff at middle C, as in the chapter before, and the Cs meet in pairs: treble C on bass C, now high C on low C, two ledger lines up, two down.

Every ledger note in this chapter is a step or a skip from low C, middle C or high C.

{{svg:five-cs}}
Teacher's tip

Five minutes at home: play the five Cs from the sea to the sky, naming each one. Then the fold partners: low C with high C, bass C with treble C, both thumbs on middle C. Then the C hop: point to a ledger note, find the nearest C, step or skip, name it, play it.

The drill for this chapter does the rest, a little every day.

My five checks

Show each one at the piano.

The ladders

  • I can point to a ledger line and say what it is
  • I can climb out of the treble staff: G, A, B, C, D, E
  • I can climb out of the bass staff: F, E, D, C, B, A

Reading fast

  • I can read the ladders between the staves, both ways
  • I can find all five Cs fast, from the sea to the sky

Common questions

What is a ledger line?

A short line above or below the staff, for one note too high or too low for the five lines. The line-space pattern carries on through it, as inside the staff. Its other spelling is "leger line".

How do you read ledger lines quickly?

First count out from the staff, one letter a step: line, space, ledger line. Then learn a few ledger notes by heart, such as high C, two ledger lines above the treble staff, and low C, two below the bass staff. Read the notes near them as a step or a skip away.

How do you draw a ledger line?

Draw it a little wider than the note head, the same distance apart as the staff lines, with no gaps back to the staff. Only as many as the note needs: a note just above or below the staff needs none, and a note in a space gets no lid on top.

Why is the same note written in two places?

Between the staves, a note can sit on ledger lines under the treble staff, on the bass staff, or on ledger lines over the bass staff. It is the same key on the piano. Writing it on each hand's own staff keeps the hands in place on the page.
