Decided along the way

  1. Sound before symbol: say and clap, then listen and sing, then play.
  2. Lessons 1 to 9: each hand plays the tune; the left hand's chord and the four Ways wait until the hands are at home (her 4 Nov 14:59). Then the chord grows one note a stage, two songs each: the root (lessons 10 to 11), two notes (12 to 13), the whole chord (14 to 15).
  3. Chords shown as a lead sheet: letters above the tune, only when the left hand plays below it. The bass clef waits for lesson 8.
  4. Moving a song to a new key comes later, white keys only at first; the five-finger rule after the hand has felt it.
  5. No teacher part in the book; the piano duet lives in the play-along.
  6. Each lesson ends with More to play: one tune that uses the lesson's things in a new way, one that peeks at the next lesson (the bridge), and a tune of your own.
  7. Before the staff (Lessons 1 to 3) the notes sit high and low on no line, the letter inside each head. From Lesson 4 the staff comes in with the letters still inside; then only the landmark notes keep them (5 to 6), then the first note of a line (7), and none from Lesson 8; page 2 keeps the boxes to write them.
  8. Classical themes get short English words of our own; melodies are checked against public scores before they are set.

Lesson numbers and ranges may move once each song is set. Files: _workspace/songbook/ on the Mac.
