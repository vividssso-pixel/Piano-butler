## 1. Theory of Music — which grades exist, and what Grades 5, 6 and above contain

### 1.1 Grades that exist

The Theory of Music syllabus runs **Grade 1 to Grade 6, then Associate and Licentiate Diplomas**. There is **no Theory of Music Grade 7 or Grade 8** anywhere in the text.

Evidence:

- Section headings in the Theory of Music syllabus (manual.txt): `GRADE 1` (line 4329 area, heading "grade 1"), `GRADE 2 ... GRADE 3` (line 4426), `GRADE 4` (line 4507), `GRADE 5` (line 4529), `GRADE 6` (line 4606), then `ASSOCIATE DIPLOMA IN MUSICOLOGY` (4596), `ASSOCIATE DIPLOMA IN HARMONY AND COUNTERPOINT` (4645), `ASSOCIATE DIPLOMA IN ORCHESTRATION AND ARRANGEMENT` (4695), `LICENTIATE DIPLOMA IN MUSICOLOGY` (4671), `LICENTIATE DIPLOMA IN HARMONY AND COUNTERPOINT` (4752), `LICENTIATE DIPLOMA IN ORCHESTRATION AND ARRANGEMENT` (4745). Nothing between Grade 6 and Associate.
- Resource list, manual.txt 4255–4266: "GRADE 1 – GRADE 3 ... GRADE 4 – GRADE 6 ... Theory of Music Integrated course and workbooks Grade 4 (AMEB, 2020), Grade 5 (AMEB, 2021) and Grade 6 (AMEB, 2024). Theory of Music Online courses Grades 4, 5 & 6 (score.ameb.edu.au). Theory of Music Analytical notes (Grades 5 & 6 only)". Then line 4282: "ASSOCIATE & LICENTIATE".
- Written-exam time table (Schedule A TABLE A), manual.txt 1405–1441: column heads are Preliminary, Grade 1 … Grade 6, Associate, Licentiate; the row "Theory of Music  60 90 120 180 180 180 180 180" has eight values = Grades 1–6 + Associate + Licentiate (no Preliminary).
- AMEB advert, manual.txt 4236–4247: "Online Theory Courses ... Grades 1 to 6 out now".
- Note the corequisite table (manual.txt 960–961) also only names "LMusA Theory of Music" and "AMusA Theory of Music" above Grade 6.

### 1.2 Grade 5 Theory of Music (manual.txt 4529–4600, right-hand column; heading "grades 4 & 5" / "grade 6 & associate")

Quoted items:

- **Pitch, Tonality, Keys and Scales** (4532–4534): "The recognition and writing of all the major and minor scales (both forms), and their key signatures."
- **Intervals** (4535–4537): "The recognition and writing of all diatonic and chromatic intervals and their inversions, in treble and bass clefs, with or without key signatures."
- **Modulation** (4541–4543): "Recognition in melodies of modulation to the dominant, subdominant, or relative major or minor."
- **Harmony** (4545–4558):
  "(a) To harmonise a melody of no more than eight bars in four-part vocal style using the following vocabulary: I II* IV V VI (*Major key only) Ib IIb IVb Vb VIb VIIb Ic (cadential) unaccented passing and auxiliary notes.
  (b) In a given melody or unfigured bass, which may modulate to closely related keys, to harmonise cadences in pianoforte or four-part vocal style, preceding each cadence by a suitable chord. First inversions may be included in cadential progressions."
- **Creative Work** (4560–4565): "To write a melody in a major or minor key up to three sharps or three flats, to a four-line stanza of poetry. The words to be correctly written below the notes. Phrase marks are to be included. Modulation is optional."
- **General Knowledge** (4567–4589):
  "(a) To show a general knowledge of the following forms, and an acquaintance with examples by the great composers: Minuet and Trio, Scherzo, Air with variations, Recitative and Aria as in Handel or Mozart.
  (b) To show a knowledge of the woodwind instruments of the symphony orchestra (flute, oboe, clarinet in Bb, clarinet in A, bassoon). A knowledge of the ranges of these instruments and the clefs used by these instruments in orchestral playing is also expected. Auxiliary instruments and instruments pitched in other keys – e.g. piccolo, cor anglais, bass clarinet, clarinet in Eb and contrabassoon will not be examined."
- **Set Works** (4591–4603): "A Minuet and Trio from a chamber work by Mozart or Haydn, and a Recitative and Aria from an oratorio by Handel will be set for study. Candidates will be asked general questions about the music and should be able to quote main themes. Candidates will need to analyse, and will be asked questions on BOTH of the following Set Works: MOZART, Divertimento in Eb major K 563, Minuet and Trio, third movement; HANDEL, Comfort ye my people and Ev'ry valley shall be exalted from Messiah HWV 56 Part 1".
- Time/rhythm, terminology, signs, transposition for Grade 5: not listed as separate Grade 5 headings in the text (the Grade 4 entry at 4507–4580 has Time and Rhythm, Transposition, Terminology, Signs; Grade 5 does not repeat them). Not in the text.

### 1.3 Grade 6 Theory of Music (manual.txt 4606–4665, left-hand column)

- **Harmony** (4608–4627): "The following vocabulary is to be used in both major and minor keys: I II III IV V VI / Ib IIb IIIb IVb Vb VIb VIIb / cadential Ic / passing: Ic, IVc, Vc / V7 V7b V7c V7d / accented and unaccented passing notes and auxiliary notes, single suspensions, Tierce de Picardie (minor keys only). Modulation within the six related keys.
  (a) To harmonise a melody or an unfigured bass in four vocal parts.
  (b) To write a part above or below a given part in simple two-part contrapuntal keyboard style."
- **Creative Work** (4629–4633): "To write a balanced melody in a major or minor key up to four sharps or flats, to a four-line stanza, with modulation. The words to be correctly written below the notes. Phrasing should be included."
- **General Knowledge** (4634–4656):
  "(a) To answer general questions on the Sonata, Symphony, Concerto, and Overture, and to refer to examples by great composers, based on the type and range of material in such reference books as the New Harvard Dictionary of Music or publications of similar scope.
  (b) To show a knowledge of First Movement or Sonata form, and to refer to a standard movement in this form in the Pianoforte Sonatas of Mozart or early Beethoven. A detailed analysis will not be required but candidates should be able to show their acquaintance with the score and with the musical effect.
  (c) To show a knowledge of the brass instruments of the symphony orchestra (horn in F, trumpet in Bb, tenor trombone, tuba in F). A knowledge of the ranges of these instruments and the clefs used by these instruments in orchestral playing is also expected. Auxiliary instruments and instruments pitched in other keys – e.g. horn in Bb, trumpet in D, bass trombone and Wagnerian tuba will not be examined."
- **Set Works** (4658–4595 right col., i.e. 4658–4665 and 4587–4595): "An overture by Handel, the first or second movement of a piano concerto by Mozart or Beethoven, and the first or second movement of a symphony by Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven or Schubert will be set for study. Candidates will be asked general questions about the music and should be able to quote main themes and where appropriate, indicate their orchestration. Candidates will need to analyse, and will be asked questions on ALL of the following Set Works: MOZART, Piano concerto No 23 in A major K 488, second movement; BEETHOVEN, Symphony No 5 in C minor Op. 67, first movement; HANDEL, Overture from Alcina HWV 34".

This matches the app's "Chords and Cadences, Grade 6" scope (V7 in four positions, passing 6/4s, decorations, six related keys, two-part writing). Items the app room does not name but the text does: cadential Ic, single suspensions, Tierce de Picardie, melody writing to a stanza with modulation, Sonata/Symphony/Concerto/Overture and sonata-form knowledge, brass instruments, and the three Set Works.

### 1.4 Above Grade 6 — the Theory diplomas (all "Total marks 100 – Pass Mark 65", three questions)

- **Associate Diploma in Musicology** (4596–4644, right col.): "(a) Analysis — To analyse a fugue from a given score ... OR given a score extract from a complex work such as a Classical symphony or Classical chamber work, to answer a series of questions regarding the analysis of the extract by discussing such aspects as harmony, texture, form and instrumentation. (b) History — To write an essay on individual works and composers chosen from any ONE of the four topics ... (i) chamber music in the nineteenth century (ii) the symphony in the eighteenth century up to and including the late symphonies of Haydn (iii) oratorio from the seventeenth to the nineteenth centuries (iv) Lied and art song in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. (c) Set Works — Two Set Works, one vocal and one instrumental ... PURCELL, Dido and Æneas; MOZART, Symphony No 25, in G minor K183".
- **Associate Diploma in Harmony and Counterpoint** (4645–4694): "Vocabulary to be used and recognised: All diatonic harmony including dominant ninths, elevenths and thirteenths as well as secondary sevenths; the principal chromatic harmony including discords on the tonic and the supertonic, augmented sixths, the Neapolitan sixth, chromatic sevenths and ninths and the other chromatic concords; notes of anticipation, appoggiaturas and changing notes; single, double and triple suspensions and retardations; accented and unaccented passing and auxiliary notes; modal harmony using all standard authentic church modes ...; modulation to related and unrelated keys. (a) Chorale Harmonisation ... in the style of J.S. Bach. (b) Counterpoint — To complete a three-part invention of not more than sixteen bars ... OR ... a two-voice canon for keyboard ... (c) Chromatic Harmony — ... three separate four-bar exercises in four-part vocal style."
- **Associate Diploma in Orchestration and Arrangement** (4695–4740): write for piano and the orchestra "as for Theory Grades 4, 5 and 6 with the addition of percussion ... OR ... jazz ensemble ... OR ... contemporary music ensemble"; (a) Scoring for Ensemble, (b) Piano Reduction, (c) Transcription/Arrangement.
- **Licentiate Diploma in Musicology** (4671–4751): (a) Analysis (Baroque harmonic analysis, or Classical/Romantic score-extract questions), (b) History essay (four topics or a list of specialist topics), (c) Set Works (one from each of two groups, e.g. Beethoven An die ferne Geliebte; Bach Orchestral Suites, Bartók Quartet No 4, Debussy Prélude à l'après-midi, Gershwin Rhapsody in Blue, Sibelius Finlandia).
- **Licentiate Diploma in Harmony and Counterpoint** (4752–4805): "A more advanced application of the harmonic and melodic resources of the Associate syllabus"; Part I (a) Stylistic Harmony (binary-form Baroque dance or étude), (b) Fugal exposition (three voices, invertible countersubject); Part II Creative Writing (instrumental vignette for six solo instruments, 3–6 minutes, within one week).
- **Licentiate Diploma in Orchestration and Arrangement** (4745–4795): full orchestra incl. piccolo, cor anglais, bass clarinet, saxophones etc.; (a) Scoring for Ensemble, (b) Piano Reduction, (c) Transcription/Arrangement.

## 2. Theory prerequisite / corequisite for each piano exam

Rule type: these are **corequisites**, not prerequisites. manual.txt 930–936:

> "8.1 A prerequisite requirement must be completed prior to an examination. 8.2 A corequisite requirement can be completed before or after the examination but no certificate can be awarded until the corequisite is satisfactorily completed. 8.3 All prerequisite and corequisite requirements, and their alternatives, are set out in Table A 1. 8.4 A degree or diploma in music from a recognised Australian or overseas tertiary institution meets prerequisite or corequisite requirement(s) for: Grades 6, 7 and 8 examinations, Certificate of Performance examinations, Associate Diploma examinations, and Licentiate Diploma examinations."

TABLE A, "AMEB COREQUISITE REQUIREMENTS*" column (manual.txt 957–979; identical in piano.txt 953–979):

| AMEB examination | AMEB corequisite | Alternate examining authorities | Recommended prior achievement |
|---|---|---|---|
| G6 Practical (979) | "Pass, G2 Theory of Music or Musicianship or Music Craft" | "Pass, Grade 2 RSL (Rockschool) Classical Theory or Grade 4 ABRSM or Trinity Theory" | — |
| G7 Practical (976) | "Pass, G3 Theory of Music or Musicianship or Music Craft" | "Pass, Grade 3 RSL ... or Grade 5 ABRSM or Trinity Theory" | — |
| G8 Practical (973) | "Pass, G4 Theory of Music or Musicianship or Music Craft" | "Pass, Grade 4 RSL ... or Grade 6 ABRSM or Trinity Theory" | — |
| Certificate of Performance (970) | "Pass, G4 Theory of Music or Musicianship or Music Craft" | "Pass, Grade 4 RSL ... or Grade 6 ABRSM or Trinity Theory" | "Pass G8 Practical" |
| AMusA Practical (967) | "Pass, G5 Theory of Music or Musicianship or Music Craft" | "Pass, Grade 5 RSL ... or Grade 7 ABRSM or Trinity Theory" | "Pass, Certificate of Performance, or credit or above in G8 Practical" |
| LMusA Practical (964) | "Pass, G6 Theory of Music or Musicianship or Music Craft" | "Pass, Grade 6 RSL ... or Grade 8 ABRSM or Trinity Theory" | "Award, AMusA Practical or equivalent" |

Footnote (979): "*Additional requirements do not apply to Piano for Leisure, Saxophone for Leisure and Singing for Leisure Examinations."

Piano syllabus restatement, piano.txt 1612–1619: "Additional requirements — Candidates are required to achieve a pass grading in Musicianship, Theory of Music or Music Craft (or equivalents) in order to pass practical examinations at Grade 6, Grade 7, Grade 8, Certificate of Performance and for the Level 3 examinations. For further information, see Regulation 8 in 'Regulations' in the front section of this Manual."

Consequence for the app: the highest theory grade any **practical** piano exam needs is **Theory Grade 6 (for LMusA)**. The app's Theory Grade 6 ceiling therefore already covers every practical corequisite. Theory Grades 5–6 are **not** needed for Grade 8 piano (Grade 4 suffices).

## 3. Grade 8 piano comprehensive — Section III

Structure, piano.txt 1025–1030 (= manual Regulation 13.1): "Comprehensive examinations in Grades Preliminary to Grade 8 (including collaborative Piano) are comprised of three sections: Section I. Technical Work; Section II. Studies and Pieces (may be referred to as the program); and Section III. Aural Tests, Sight-Reading, and General Knowledge."

Grade 8 piano entry, piano.txt 3315–3324: "GRADE 8 (SOLO) 9948 — Please refer to the syllabus objectives at the beginning of this level. Section I. Technical work — All technical work requirements are set out in the AMEB publication Piano Technical work Level 2 (2018). Section II. Studies and pieces — Candidates must prepare FOUR works, one from each of Lists A, B, C and D for presentation at examination." (No Extra Lists at Grade 8: piano.txt 1623–1624 "Candidates are required to present two Extra List works in addition to List Pieces for all grades from Grade 2 to Grade 7, both inclusive." and 2686 "Extra lists (Grades 5, 6, & 7 only)".) Section III heading at 3457.

### 3.1 Aural tests — TABLE B, Grade 8 row (manual.txt 716–725; duplicated at 1668–1677; piano.txt 712–721)

> "Grade 8 HARMONY: The examiner having sounded any major or minor triad either in root position or an inversion, or any diminished triad in root position played within the limits of an octave, the candidate will recognise the major, minor or diminished triad, stating its position (in the case of a major or minor triad).
> HARMONY: To recognise any of the four principal cadences in a major key as they occur in a short piece played by the examiner, the tonic chord being first sounded.
> MEMORY: Approximately one and a half minutes being allowed to memorise a three- or four-bar melodic phrase from a printed copy away from the instrument, the candidate will then play or sing the phrase from memory. The examiner will sound the keynote before the phrase is memorised.
> PITCH: The examiner will play on a piano a two-part phrase with a few passing notes, and the candidate will hum, sing or play from memory, the lower part."

General notes (manual.txt 633–641): "Aural Tests in Grades other than Preliminary are played twice. In the case of tests specified in more than one Grade, the difficulty of the examples given to the candidates are adjusted to the standards of the respective Grades."

The app's `data_aural_ameb.js` G8 entry (lines 92–100) reproduces these four tests verbatim.

### 3.2 Sight-reading (manual.txt 728–731; piano.txt 2702–2712)

> "22. Sight-Reading Test Requirements — For candidates in comprehensive practical examinations only. Sight-Reading tests apply to Grades 1 – 8 and are set within the limits of the technical standard of the Grade."

Piano Level 2 objectives (piano.txt 2702–2712): "Sight-reading — Through the performance of a previously unseen piece of music, the candidate is required to demonstrate: Accuracy of notes, time and rhythm; Fluency at the tempo indicated; Dynamics, articulation and style as indicated; Use of the sustaining pedal where appropriate."

No further Grade-8-specific detail (keys, length) is in the text.

### 3.3 General knowledge — TABLE C, Grade 8 (manual.txt 736–788; duplicated at 1680–1700+; piano.txt 736–800)

Common rules (736–749):
> "Candidates can expect to be asked: At least one question about each List piece presented; Between 6 and 10 questions in total; In Level 1 ... questions are asked from the candidate's part ...; In Levels 2 and 3, examinations on solo instruments and voice, questions are asked from the accompaniment part (not the candidate's part). In Grade 7 and beyond, candidates are asked about the composer. Candidates should interpret 'period' as an historical time frame; 'style' as the features, attributes and characteristics of the music or period (for example texture, phrasing, articulation, use of dynamics, motif, harmony, ornamentation, tempo etc.)."

Row "Level 2 — Grades 7, 8 & Certificate of Performance" (774–789):
> "By referring to the score of each List piece, candidates can be asked to:
> 1. Name and/or explain any notes, rests, signs, terms, its title and its key/tonality;
> 2. Identify (on the appropriate score) any key changes that occur, or explain other type(s) of tonal organisation(s) e.g. atonal, pentatonic, whole tone where applicable; and
> 3. Explain form, through a detailed structural analysis. Where the piece is a movement from a larger work: • Grades 7 & 8: Brief knowledge of its relationship to the other movements in the work is expected. • Certificate of Performance: Knowledge of its relationship to the other movements in the work, including a description of the other movements, is required.
> Either from the List piece score, or by reference to general characteristics not apparent on the score itself, candidates can be asked to demonstrate:
> 1. Knowledge of the period and style appropriate to the piece;
> 2. Knowledge of other genres typical of the period but not necessarily instrument-specific is also expected, e.g. orchestral music, choral music; and
> 3. Knowledge of the composers of each of the pieces being presented, in particular relating to their works and musical influences, is expected."

For comparison, the Grades 5 & 6 row (765–773) limits form questions to a named list ("Binary, Ternary, Rondo, Theme and Variation types, First Movement (Sonata), Fugue, Minuet (Scherzo) and Trio, Through Composed") and asks only to "Name the period ... and state the time frame" and "Discuss style". Grade 7/8 adds: detailed structural analysis of any form, relationship to other movements, non-tonal organisation (atonal, pentatonic, whole tone), other genres of the period (orchestral, choral), and composer biography/works/influences.

Scores rule (piano.txt 424 / manual 7. Memorisation (c)): "Candidates playing from memory must bring their music to the examination for the General Knowledge section." And (piano.txt 455) "Candidates must remove all marks from their music which refer to general knowledge information, including the meaning of terms, signs etc."

### 3.4 Technical work (for completeness)

## 4. Certificate of Performance, AMusA, LMusA (piano)

Structure rules, piano.txt 1035–1040 (= manual Regulation 13.3–13.4):
> "13.3 Examination in the Certificate of Performance is comprised of two sections: Section I. Studies and Pieces (may be referred to as the program); and Section II. General Knowledge.
> 13.4 Associate Diploma of Music and Licentiate Diploma of Music examinations are comprised of two sections: Section I. Pieces (may be referred to as the program); and Section II. General Knowledge."

Hence for all three: **technical work — no; aural tests — no; sight-reading — no; general knowledge — yes.** (Sight-reading "apply to Grades 1 – 8" only, manual 731; TABLE B aural is "Preliminary to Grade 8", 1595.) The Level definitions (manual 865–866): "Level 2 is Grade 5 – Certificate of Performance and Level 3 comprises Associate and Licentiate Diplomas."

Examiners: manual.txt 1371–1373: "One (1) examiner present at all Level 1 and 2 examinations ...; Two (2) examiners present at Diploma level examinations (Associate and Licentiate)".

### 4.1 Certificate of Performance (piano.txt 3524–3552; 1609–1619)

- Objectives (3531–3534): "Candidates must demonstrate musicality, maturity, conviction and confidence in the performance of a well-balanced programme that covers a variety of technical skills and musical emphases."
- Section I. Pieces (3541–3552): "This syllabus is intended to be prepared as a musical performance. The examination will last 45 minutes. Candidates should prepare a balanced and varied programme of not less than 25 minutes and not more than 35 minutes, including appropriate gaps between the pieces. One work must be chosen from each of Lists A, B, C, and D, with the remainder of the programme taken from Lists A, C or D at the candidate's discretion. The choice of works for the programme as a whole should ensure a diversity of at least three styles and/or historical periods."
- Section II. General knowledge (3665–3669, end of the CoP lists): "The candidate will be required to answer questions according to the guidelines set out in 'General Requirements for Practical Examinations – 23. General Knowledge Test Requirements' in the front section of this Manual." → i.e. the "Grades 7, 8 & Certificate of Performance" row in §3.3 above, with the stricter CoP clause: "Knowledge of its relationship to the other movements in the work, including a description of the other movements, is required."
- Written component / program notes / viva voce: not in the text for the Certificate of Performance.
- Memory: no requirement stated; general rule piano.txt 421–424 (b) "credit will be given to candidates who elect to perform from memory".
- Theory corequisite: Grade 4 (see §2). Recommended prior: "Pass G8 Practical".

### 4.2 AMusA — Associate Diploma (piano.txt 3673–3763)

- Level 3 objectives (3677–3686): "The candidate must demonstrate an advanced level of technical accomplishment, a mature musical understanding and the ability to feel and project the changing mood and character of the music that is performed. The repertoire should be chosen carefully so that the program fits within the prescribed timings, achieves the required balance and stylistic diversity and covers a variety of technical skills and musical emphases."
- Section I. Pieces, what is assessed (3703–3722): "A concept of the works as a whole, performed with an understanding of the structure and the musical content ...; Mastery of technical and musical elements ...; The maintenance of unity and continuity in multi-movement works, including appropriate timing between, and linking of movements; Command of contemporary techniques appropriate to the instrument; Understanding of performance conventions relevant to the works being performed, and an ability to differentiate between a range of music styles; A sense of professional presentation, with attention to posture, appearance and normal performance conventions; Performance from memory according to the requirements of the specific syllabus."
- Program (3752–3767): "Duration should be not less than 25 minutes and not more than 40 minutes, including appropriate gaps between pieces. Repeats may be performed at the candidate's discretion ... All da capo directions must be observed. One work must be chosen from List A. At least three works must be chosen from any of Lists B, C, or D. While it is not necessary for the program to contain a work from every list, the choice of repertoire must ensure diversity, covering at least three different styles and/or historical periods. Additional works needed to fulfil the time requirements may be selected from Lists B, C, or D." (List A = complete sonatas/suites: C.P.E. Bach, Beethoven Op. 2/1, 10/1, 10/2, 13, 14/2, 22, 26, 27/1, 28, 78, Clementi, Haydn, Mozart, Schubert; List B = Bach Preludes & Fugues, Partita/English Suite movements, etc., 3769+.)
- Section II. General knowledge (3724–3750): "Candidates will be expected to demonstrate a mature understanding of the structure, style and historical background of the repertoire that is performed and to use this as a basis for discussing the interpretive choices that they make. Questions, which will be asked from the full piano score, will focus on the following areas: Terminology, including the significance of titles and the meaning of all markings contained in the score. Composers: relevant biographical information, sources of influence, impact upon later generations of composers and repertoire output (both in the discipline being examined and more broadly). Historical and stylistic context: how each work relates to the period from which it arises, considering both its compositional style and the performance conventions of the time. Structure: formal layout, keys, melodic/harmonic language and the identification of climax points. For instrumentalists, the construction and development of the instrument, with special consideration given to any implications this may have upon the repertoire that is performed. Candidates must provide the examiners with clear, unmarked copies of all scores." (Same five areas in manual TABLE C Level 3 row, 790–799.)
- Program notes: manual.txt 800–801 (TABLE C, Level 3): "Candidates may be required to prepare program notes for the examiners for Section I of the exam, as set out in the relevant Syllabus." The Piano syllabus itself does not set out any program-notes requirement for AMusA — **not in the text** for piano. The words "viva voce" appear in the Manual only for the teaching awards (CTMusA/ATMusA, manual.txt 2022–2031, 2473–2478), not for piano diplomas.
- Memory: AMusA has no memory requirement in the text (only "Performance from memory according to the requirements of the specific syllabus", and the piano syllabus names only Licentiate, see 4.3).
- Scores: piano.txt 1629–1631 "Copies of works presented in the Associate and Licentiate examinations must be provided for the use of examiners."
- Theory corequisite: Theory Grade 5 (see §2). Recommended prior: CoP pass, or credit or above in Grade 8.

### 4.3 LMusA — Licentiate Diploma (piano.txt 3896–3920)

- Program (3896–3920): "A concert standard of performance is expected in this examination. Duration should be not less than 35 minutes and not more than 50 minutes, including appropriate gaps between pieces. Repeats may be performed at the candidate's discretion ... - Program notes / written component / viva voce: not in the text for piano (same Manual caveat as 4.2).
- Theory corequisite: Theory Grade 6 (see §2). Recommended prior: AMusA award.

piano.txt 1304–1345: Fellowship recital "approximately 80 minutes' music content", "For pianists the entire programme must be presented from memory with the exception of contemporary works." No syllabus (manual 9.2). Out of scope for the app.

## 5. Gap list — what a Grade 8 / diploma candidate needs that the app does not yet have

Legend: **[exam-required]** = stated in the syllabus as examined; **[useful, not examined]** = supports preparation but no exam section tests it directly.

### Grade 8 comprehensive

- **General knowledge at Grade 7/8 depth for Grade 8 pieces** — detailed structural analysis of the candidate's own four List pieces, relationship of a movement to the rest of its work, tonal organisation incl. atonal/pentatonic/whole-tone, other genres of the period (orchestral, choral), composer's life, works and influences (manual 774–789). The app has "Knowing Your Pieces, Grades 7 and 8" and a viva-voce page; what the text requires and the app should be checked for: (a) questions are asked "from the accompaniment part" wording is irrelevant for solo piano, but (b) the Grade 7/8 row explicitly adds composer knowledge and "other genres typical of the period". **[exam-required]** — depth of existing room, not a new room.
- **Grade 8 aural: diminished triad recognition (root position) alongside major/minor with position; all four principal cadences; 3–4 bar memory test in 90 seconds; sing/play the *lower* part of a two-part phrase with passing notes** (manual 716–725). The app lists these in `data_aural_ameb.js`; Ear Training 8 should be verified to drill each. **[exam-required]**
- **Sight-reading at Grade 8 standard** (manual 731; piano 2702–2712 criteria: notes/time, fluency at the marked tempo, dynamics/articulation/style, sustaining pedal). The website generator exists; the drills app has no sight-reading room. **[exam-required]** — covered on the website, not in drills.
- **Theory beyond Grade 6 for Grade 8** — none required; Grade 8 needs only **Theory Grade 4** (manual 973). The app's Theory Grade 6 ceiling already exceeds every practical corequisite. **[not required]**

### Certificate of Performance

- **No technical work, aural or sight-reading** (Regulation 13.3). The gap is on the other side: nothing in the app addresses **programme-building** (25–35 min, one from each of A–D, three styles/periods, 45-minute exam) (piano 3541–3552). **[useful, not examined as a test; the programme itself is Section I]**
- **General knowledge with the stricter CoP clause**: "Knowledge of its relationship to the other movements in the work, including a description of the other movements, is required" (manual 776–778). Not in the app's Grade 7/8 room. **[exam-required]**

### AMusA / LMusA

- **Level 3 general knowledge, five areas** (piano 3724–3750): terminology incl. significance of titles; composer biography, influences, impact on later composers, output; historical/stylistic context *and performance conventions of the time*; structure incl. keys, melodic/harmonic language and **climax points**; **construction and development of the piano and its effect on repertoire**. The last two are new relative to anything in the app. Also "use this as a basis for discussing the interpretive choices that they make". **[exam-required]**
- **History of the piano as an instrument** (fortepiano → modern; implications for repertoire) — explicitly examined at Level 3. The app's timeline of composers does not cover instrument construction. **[exam-required at diploma level]**
- **Programme construction for diplomas** — AMusA 25–40 min, LMusA 35–50 min, one List A work (complete sonata/suite) + at least three from B/C/D, three styles/periods, repeats optional, da capos observed (piano 3752–3767, 3896–3920). A piece-finder exists on the website; a diploma programme-planner with timings does not. **[useful but not examined]**
- **Memory: LMusA one complete work from memory** (piano 3917, 1700–1703). **[exam-required, LMusA only]** — not an app feature as such; the Grade 7/8 aural "memory" tests are the only related drill.
- **Clean unmarked scores for examiners** (piano 3748–3750, 1629–1631). **[exam-required; admin]**
- **Program notes / viva voce**: the Manual says candidates "may be required to prepare program notes ... as set out in the relevant Syllabus" (manual 800), but the Piano syllabus sets none → **not in the text** for piano; do not build a program-notes requirement into the app as "exam-required". "Viva voce" occurs only for teaching diplomas.
- **Theory corequisites: AMusA needs Theory Grade 5, LMusA needs Theory Grade 6** (manual 964–968). The app already stops at Theory Grade 6, so this is covered — but the app's Grade 5/6 theory rooms would need to include everything the Theory Grade 5/6 papers set, not only harmony: melody writing to a stanza (G5 up to 3 sharps/flats, G6 with modulation), forms (G5 Minuet & Trio, Scherzo, Air with variations, Recitative & Aria; G6 Sonata, Symphony, Concerto, Overture, sonata form), orchestral instruments (G5 woodwind, G6 brass, with ranges and clefs), the Set Works (G5 Mozart K 563 Minuet & Trio + Handel "Comfort ye"/"Ev'ry valley"; G6 Mozart K 488 ii + Beethoven Symphony 5 i + Handel Alcina Overture), G5 all scales/key signatures and all diatonic + chromatic intervals with inversions, G5 modulation recognition. **[exam-required for the theory corequisite, not for the practical]** — the app's "Chords and Cadences, Grade 6" covers the harmony vocabulary only; there is no Theory Grade 5 room at all in the Grade 6–8 floors as described.
- **Theory diplomas (Associate/Licentiate Musicology, Harmony & Counterpoint, Orchestration)** — exist (manual 4596–4805) but are **not required** for any practical piano exam; chromatic harmony (augmented 6ths, Neapolitan, 9ths/11ths/13ths, suspensions), fugue analysis, Bach chorale style, modes. **[useful but not examined for practical candidates]**

### Grade 7 (noted while checking)

- Grade 7 aural (manual 707–715): major/minor triad with position; Perfect/Plagal/Interrupted cadences; 2-bar memory test in one minute; sing higher or lower part of a six-note two-part phrase. Theory corequisite Grade 3 (manual 976). Extra Lists still required at Grade 7 (piano 1623–1624), not at Grade 8.
