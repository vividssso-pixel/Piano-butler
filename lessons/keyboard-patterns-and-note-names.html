Keyboard patterns and note names

The piano looks like 88 different keys, but it is one small pattern, repeated. Learn the pattern first, and every key on the piano tells you its own name.

Written by Clara Sohyun Park · Updated 9 October 2026

From the teacher

The most common stumble is recognising the pattern and the letter names, and especially the moment of crossing into F, G and A at the group of three black keys.

That is why I teach the pattern first and the names second. Once a student sees the black-key groups and the white keys that belong to each one, the note names have somewhere to live, and later the finger patterns of the scales come much more easily. Younger children need to live in the pattern for a while; older beginners usually see it straight away, and can go on to the names in the same lesson.

Small house, big house

The black keys are the roofs of the houses. Two black keys together are the roof of a small house; three are the roof of a big house. Small, big, small, big: the same two houses repeat all the way up the piano. Look for the shapes, not the single keys.

  • 2 black keys = the small house's roof.
  • 3 black keys = the big house's roof.
  • Low on the left, high on the right. The street climbs from the bear's low end to the bird's high end.

The white keys of each house

A white key is never just anywhere: each one lives under a roof. Under the small roof live 3 white keys (the left door, the inside and the right door); under the big roof live 4. Three and four make seven, and after seven the street starts again.

This grouping is the key idea of the chapter. A student who sees it finds every note name from the pattern, instead of counting up from the bottom of the piano.

The seven letter names

Music uses just seven letters. C D E live at the small house, F G A B at the big one. Learn the two left doors and you can walk to every other name.

  • C is the small house's left door.
  • F is the big house's left door.
  • After G comes A again. There is no H.

The whole piano and middle C

A real piano has 88 keys, 52 white and 36 black, yet it is still only the two houses: 7 small ones and 7 big ones. At the very bottom lives one lonely black key with no house at all.

Count the small houses from the bottom up to the 4th one: its left door is middle C. To the left of it live the low voices, to the right the high voices. Middle C is the door where they meet.

Play on the patterns

The first tunes need no written notes at all: just the black-key roofs and fingers 2, 3 and 4. The right hand plays on the big roof, the left hand's 2 and 3 on the small roof. Fingers 1 and 5 come later, with the C position.

More tunes that live on the black keys: Mary Had a Little Lamb, Old MacDonald, and Rain Rain Go Away.

Teacher's tip

I teach legato like a relay baton. Hold the key down until the next one plays, and the moment the new note sounds, let the old one go. No gap between the two sounds, and no blur of both held together.

For first tunes I reach for Hot Cross Buns, Mary Had a Little Lamb and Old MacDonald: famous, free to use, and everybody already knows how they go.

My five checks

Show, don't tell: each one is shown on the keyboard.

On the keyboard

  • I can find a small house and a big house anywhere
  • I can say each house's white keys: 3 and 4
  • I can name any white key

And playing

  • I can find middle C, just left of the 4th small house
  • I can play Hot Cross Buns legato with 2 3 4

Common questions

How do you find middle C on a piano?

Count the groups of two black keys from the bottom of the piano. At the 4th group, the white key just to its left is middle C.

Why learn the black keys before the note names?

The black keys come in groups of two and three, and every white key belongs to one of those groups: three at the group of two, four at the group of three. Once that pattern is seen, every note name can be found from it instead of by counting.

How many keys does a piano have?

88: 52 white keys and 36 black keys. The same pattern of two and three black keys repeats seven times, with one extra black key at the very bottom.
