First, a quick test. Play the key on the far left. Now the key on the far right. Hear that? Left is low. Right is high. That never changes.

Now look at the black keys. They come in little groups. A group of two. Then a group of three. Two, three. Two, three. All the way along. That pattern is your map.

Here's the secret. Every group has a first white key, just to its left. Next to the group of two, the first key is C. Next to the group of three, the first key is F. So C starts the two, and F starts the three.

Let's try it. Find any group of two black keys. Put a finger on the white key just to its left. That's C. Walk up: C, D, E. E is the last one in that family.

Now find a group of three black keys. The white key just to its left is F. Walk up: F, G, A, B. B finishes that family. And guess what? Right after B, you're back at C again.

So, say Clara wants to play G. Does she start at C and count, C, D, E, F, G? She could, but it's slow. Instead, she looks for three black keys, finds F, and steps once. G. Done. Find the group, find its first key, then walk on.

Now let's meet the famous Cs. Find the C nearest the middle of the piano. That's Middle C. One C lower is Bass C. One C higher is Treble C. Further out, you'll find Low C and High C. Five Cs, five names.

Before we finish, here's a tune you can play with no white keys at all. Right hand. Put fingers two, three and four on a group of three black keys. Ready? Four, three, two. Four, three, two. Two, two, two, two. Three, three, three, three. Four, three, two. That's Hot Cross Buns.

Your mission this week: close your eyes, slide your hand along the keys, and feel for the bumps. Two bumps, and the key to their left is C. Three bumps, and the key to their left is F. See you in the next chapter.
